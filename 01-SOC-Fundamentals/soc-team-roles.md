# SOC Team Roles and Responsibilities

## 1. Introduction

A Security Operations Centre (SOC) consists of a team of security professionals who work together to monitor systems, detect security threats, investigate suspicious activities, and respond to security incidents.

Each team member has specific responsibilities. Their collaboration helps protect an organisation's systems, networks, and sensitive information.

## 2. Key SOC Team Roles

### 2.1 Security Analyst

Security analysts monitor security events, investigate alerts, and identify potentially malicious activities.

Their responsibilities include:

- Monitoring security alerts generated by SIEM tools.
- Reviewing logs and suspicious activities.
- Checking Indicators of Compromise (IoCs).
- Validating whether an alert is a true positive or false positive.
- Escalating confirmed incidents when further investigation is required.
- Documenting investigation findings.

### 2.2 Security Engineer

Security engineers work with security technologies and infrastructure to help maintain the organisation's security.

Their responsibilities may include:

- Implementing and maintaining security tools.
- Configuring security solutions.
- Supporting security monitoring infrastructure.
- Improving security configurations and defences.

### 2.3 SOC Manager

The SOC Manager oversees SOC operations and coordinates the security team.

Responsibilities generally include:

- Managing SOC activities and priorities.
- Coordinating team members.
- Monitoring operational performance.
- Supporting incident management and reporting.
- Ensuring that security processes are followed.

### 2.4 Incident Responder

An incident responder handles security incidents after they have been detected and escalated.

The main objective is to limit the damage and restore affected systems to normal operations.

Key responsibilities include:

- Responding to confirmed security incidents.
- Following established incident response procedures.
- Containing affected systems to prevent further spread.
- Supporting mitigation and recovery activities.
- Documenting incident details and actions taken.

### 2.5 Threat Analyst

Threat analysts collect and analyse information about potential threats and attacks that could affect an organisation.

Their responsibilities include:

- Gathering information about emerging threats.
- Studying attacker activities and techniques.
- Sharing relevant threat information with the security team.
- Helping the organisation strengthen its defences against potential attacks.

## 3. SOC Analyst L1 and L2 Escalation

SOC teams commonly use different analyst levels to handle security alerts and incidents.

### SOC Analyst L1 – Initial Monitoring and Triage

L1 analysts are generally responsible for the initial review of security alerts.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Monitoring alerts generated by SIEM tools.
- Reviewing relevant logs and events.
- Checking Indicators of Compromise (IoCs).
- Determining whether an alert is a true positive or false positive.
- Recording findings and raising tickets.
- Escalating confirmed or complex incidents to L2.

### SOC Analyst L2 – Investigation and Analysis

L2 analysts handle incidents that require deeper investigation.

Typical responsibilities may include:

- Performing detailed incident investigations.
- Analysing suspicious activities and related evidence.
- Determining the scope and potential impact of an incident.
- Coordinating with incident responders when required.
- Providing investigation findings and recommendations.

**Note:** Exact responsibilities vary between organisations and SOC operating models.

## 4. How SOC Team Members Work Together

A typical escalation process can follow this sequence:

1. SIEM tools collect and analyse logs from different systems.
2. Security alerts are generated when suspicious activities are detected.
3. The L1 analyst reviews the alert and checks relevant indicators.
4. The analyst determines whether the alert is genuine or a false positive.
5. If further investigation is required, the analyst raises a ticket and escalates it to L2.
6. L2 investigates the incident in greater detail.
7. The incident may be handed over to the incident response team for response and recovery.
8. The incident and actions taken are documented for future reference and improvement.

## 5. Key Takeaway

A SOC depends on collaboration between different security professionals. Security analysts monitor and investigate alerts, security engineers support security technologies, managers coordinate operations, incident responders handle detected incidents, and threat analysts provide information about potential threats.

Understanding these roles is important for anyone preparing to work as a SOC Analyst, particularly at the L1 level, where alert monitoring, initial investigation, documentation, and escalation are key responsibilities.
